Our Approach: From Charity to Administrative Empowerment

Ankur Trust was established on the principle that vulnerable indigenous communities do not require passive charity; they require structural access to their constitutional rights, land security, and public administrative systems. Focusing heavily on the Katkari Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Group (PVTG) and Thakar families, our work dismantles systemic barriers—such as paperlessness, illegal land dispossession, and informal debt bondage—by equipping community members with the legal literacy and documentation needed to self-advocate.

  1. 01

    Rights-Assertion over Dependency

    Building local capacity so Gram Sabhas and indigenous families independently file administrative claims, track welfare applications, and access state entitlements.

  2. 02

    Constructive State Collaboration

    Functioning as an effective administrative bridge between isolated hamlets and district departments without resorting to adversarial rhetoric.

  3. 03

    Women-Led Economic Autonomy

    Centering financial management around indigenous women through zero-interest revolving capital models that eliminate reliance on predatory moneylenders.

  4. 04

    Intergenerational Educational Continuity

    Providing holistic residential care, nutrition, and foundational learning to tribal children to break cycles of seasonal distress migration.
